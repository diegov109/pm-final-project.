  • Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps: Wanderer: wants something good to watch with minimal effort, and is blocked by a trending-heavy library of mid-tier titles.
  • Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes: Wanderers can’t quickly tell what is worth watching, so they stop browsing before they start a satisfying session.
  • Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?: Deeper first-month engagement and lower Wanderer churn, protecting recurring revenue from 41% of the base.
  • Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing: 30+ minute session rate among Spotlight-exposed Wanderers
  • Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base: Power User sessions per week (4.8×) and overall DAU (1.49M), so curation doesn’t hurt the heavy-use base.

Competitive Analysis & Journey Map

Persona & Future-State Journey, Module 2

  • Scenario: StreamLine Spotlight (B2C)

Persona

  • Role: Wanderer, a casual subscriber (41% of the base) who watches about 1.1 sessions a week
  • Goal: Find something worth watching quickly, with no effort
  • Friction: Scrolls trending rows of mid-tier titles, can’t tell what’s good, and leaves before pressing play

Current workaround (the status quo to beat)

  • External tools: Texts to friends, “what to watch” lists and review sites, social media, and a competitor’s curated picks
  • The process: 1.Open StreamLine, scroll the trending rows, and find nothing convincing.

2.Leave the app to ask a friend or check a “what to watch” list.

3.Search StreamLine to see whether the pick is even available.

4.Start watching, or give up and close the app.

  • Core frustration: The moment after scrolling when nothing signals what’s good, so they have to leave the app to decide.
  • The evidence: The data backs it up: 61% of their plays come from trending, searched-then-played fell from 41% to 34%, and only 11% of sessions reach 30+ minutes.

Future-state journey map

1. Stage 1, Open. Opens the app, sees Spotlight picks first → trusts what’s worth watching right away, Hopeful but skeptical. No quality signal

2. Stage 2, Scan. Spots Hidden Gem badges on rail titles → sees quality at a glance , Curious. Can’t tell what’s good

3. Stage 3, Reads the “Why You’ll Love This” reason → chooses in seconds without leaving. Confident ,Decision paralysis

4. Stage 4, Watch and Return. Presses play, then returns for fresh picks → stays past 30 minutes and has a reason to come back, Satisfied. Early drop-off

Competitive advantages

1.It’s inside the app. The Wanderer doesn’t leave to decide or wonder whether the pick is available.

2.It shows quality at a glance. A hand-picked rail and a badge replace guessing and friends’ texts, and Spotlight cohorts keep 74 to 79% at Month 1 versus 63 to 68%.

3.It takes no effort. The picks refresh without setup, quizzes, or searching, which suits someone who wants zero work to find a show.

Experimentation Plan

Parameters

Parameter · Decision

Feature under test · A1 Spotlight Curated Rail: a hand-picked homepage rail that bypasses the algorithm

Persona · Wanderer: casual subscriber (41% of the base), about 1.1 sessions a week, 61% of plays from trending

Expected outcome · More Wanderer sessions reach 30+ minutes because they find something worth watching

Primary success metric · 30+ minute session rate among Wanderers

Baseline rate · 11% of sessions reach 30+ minutes (down from 19% six months ago)

Guardrail metric · Power User sessions per week (4.8)

Guardrail boundary · Must not drop more than 5% (below about 4.6 sessions a week)

Second guardrail · Overall daily active users (1.49M) must not drop more than 2%

Minimum Detectable Effect · +2 pts (11% to 13%)

Sample size per arm · 4,144

Traffic split · 50/50

Test duration · 14 days (two full weekly cycles)

Significance threshold · p < 0.05 (95%)

Control vs. Variant

  • Control (A): The homepage today. Wanderers scroll rows that are 61% trending, can’t tell what’s worth watching, and leave before pressing play. Only 11% of sessions reach 30+ minutes, and only 34% of searches end in a play.
  • Variant (B): The same homepage with one hand-picked Spotlight Curated Rail added as the first row. Nothing else changes.
  • Held constant (isolation check): App version, the recommendation engine and every other row, search, notifications, onboarding, pricing, the content catalog, and the existing Spotlight entry points. The other Now features (A2 label and A3 badge) must not ship to either arm during the test, or you won’t know which one caused the lift.

GTM Launch Plan & Success Dashboard

GTM Launch Plan, StreamLine (B2C)

Field · Value

Feature · Spotlight Curated Rail: a hand-picked homepage rail that bypasses the algorithm.

Goal · Engagement

Launch tier · M, Targeted

Goal & Audience

  • Goal: Engagement, Wanderers already subscribe, so awareness isn’t the problem. Only 11% of sessions reach 30+ minutes, and the issue is that they can’t tell what’s worth watching.
  • Target audience: Primary is Wanderers (41% of the base, about 1.1 sessions a week, 61% of plays from trending). Secondary is Casual Browsers (37%, a 14-point churn improvement when exposed to Spotlight). Power Users are left out of the comms so the guardrail holds.

Launch Tier

  • M, Targeted, The audience is existing subscribers, so there’s no need for press or events. The rail is on the homepage, so the cost of silence is low. The only risk is that Wanderers don’t notice it, or don’t read it as quality, so a light set of comms is enough.

Channels

1. Owned: The in-app rail with a short first-session callout, plus an email and push to inactive Wanderers. It reaches the exact audience at zero media cost.

2. Earned: Seed the picks with critics and “what to watch” creators. It speaks to the “can’t tell what’s good” problem, and a pick someone else vouches for is easy to share.

3. Paid: Retargeting ads on social to lapsed Wanderers and Casual Browsers, using a 15-second clip of the rail.

Enablement & Assets

A one-pager for Support on what Spotlight is and how titles are picked, plus macro replies. Assets: in-app callout copy, launch email and push, a 15-second screen recording, and a feature flag for rollout. The gap to flag is an editorial owner to refresh the picks every week.

Ownership, Budget & Timeline

  • Ownership & budget: PM owns the plan and post-launch metrics. Editorial lead owns weekly picks (new ongoing time, so a resourcing ask). Marketing owns email, push, and paid (small test budget needed). Design owns the callout and video. Engineers owns the rollout flag. Support is briefed, with no extra budget. Replace the roles with real names.
  • Timeline: Weeks 1 to 2 (beta): Run the 50/50 A/B test and seed critics and creators.

Week 3 (launch): If the test meets the ship criteria, roll out to 100%, with in-app callout, email, push, and paid retargeting live.

Week 4 Onward: monitor, collect feedback, and decide on iteration.

Success Metrics

  • Metrics: -30+ minute session rate among Wanderers: from 11% to 13% or higher.

-Share of Wanderer plays from curated content: from 18% today, rising.

-Month-1 retention of exposed Wanderers, compared with the 63 to 68% Full Library baseline.

  • Bad signal to watch for: People click the rail but sessions stay short. That means the picks don’t match what Wanderers want, and the rail is only adding another place to scroll. Also watch for Power User sessions per week dropping below 4.6.
  • Likely post-launch decision: Double down if the 30+ minute rate reaches 13% and the guardrail holds, then extend to Casual Browsers and add A2 and A3. Iterate if the lift is positive but under 2 points. Deprioritize if there’s no lift after the 14-day read.
Module 6

Individual Insights

Where the work was hardest, the moment my thinking changed, and what I learned.

Friction points

The hardest part was that the data didn’t always say what I expected. The funnel looked fine at the top, but the real leak was after people pressed play: 29% started, and only 11% reached 30+ minutes. I worked through it by checking each snapshot against my persona’s pain before moving on, and by comparing relative drops, not just points. Scoring features was also hard, because the AI’s first pass sounded confident but ignored my persona’s problem. I fixed that by overriding its scores with my own data. And keeping the same persona and metric from module to module took discipline, because every lab was easy to do in isolation and harder to keep consistent.

Aha moment

The AI is only as useful as the question I give it. When I gave it a user, a goal, and a framework, the output got sharp. When I left those out, I got generic answers. It was fast, but I still had to decide what to build and why. The interview quote “The algorithm just wants me to keep scrolling” was the moment it clicked, because it showed that adding more or smarter recommendations could make the problem worse.

Key learnings

-Lowest value isn’t lowest priority. Wanderers have the lowest LTV ($8.40), but they show the biggest churn improvement (22 points) when exposed to Spotlight, so they were the best segment to target.
-Saying no is part of the roadmap. Watch Party (Sales) and the Filter Engine (Engineering) both sounded reasonable, but neither helped my persona find something worth watching, so I cut them.
-Test one change at a time. I held back the other Now features so that any lift in the A/B test could be traced to the rail.
-Match the metric to the goal. Wanderers already subscribe, so I launched for Engagement and judged success on 30+ minute sessions, not reach.
-A prototype finds gaps a PRD hides. Building it showed my PRD never said how the user gets back to the rail.
